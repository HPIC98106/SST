One change unlocks three of them group A

These three look like separate features and are not. Each needs the same single thing: a QuickBooks dimension that says which grant or project a transaction belongs to, applied when the entry is made. That is the conversation with Rachel. Agreeing to it once buys all three, which is a much better trade than it looks from the outside.

A word on projections

Should the dashboard forecast, or only report?

Not a drawing. A question.

Everything on the dashboard today is a read: it says what happened and where the figure came from. Projected membership revenue would be the first number on the page that is not a fact, but a model with assumptions inside it.

That is not a reason to refuse it. It is a reason to show the assumptions next to it, the same way the phase target now prints where its figure came from. A forecast nobody can interrogate is how a dashboard starts being argued with instead of believed.

SuggestStart with dues actually recorded and the renewal rate. Add the projection once the board agrees what it assumes.

What we are actually asking the board

Not "do these look good." They all look good; that is what a drawing is for. The useful questions are narrower.

  1. Which one would you actually open? Every panel here costs real effort to keep honest, and a panel nobody reads is worse than none.
  2. Is the bookkeeping change worth it? Three of these five need one habit changed in QuickBooks. That single decision is the difference between this staying a grant tracker and becoming a financial picture.
  3. What does "active member" mean? The membership panel is buildable as soon as somebody answers that. It is a policy question, not a technical one.
  4. What is missing from this sheet? Five ideas came from one person. The board has been running this organization for a lot longer.
